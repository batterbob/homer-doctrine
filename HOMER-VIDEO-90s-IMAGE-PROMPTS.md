# Image prompts — 90s intro video (6 stills)

For the stills + voiceover route. One image per scene of
`HOMER-VIDEO-SCRIPT-90s.md`.

## HOW TO USE
1. Paste **STYLE BLOCK + the scene prompt** together, once per scene. The style block
   must be **identical every time** — that repetition is what makes six separately
   generated images look like one video.
2. **Generate no text inside the images.** Image models garble words. Every label,
   caption, and the end card get added in your slideshow/editor afterward, where they
   will be crisp and correct.
3. Aspect ratio **16:9**. Generate 2–3 variants per scene and keep the calmest one.
4. If a model drifts in style by scene 4–5, re-paste the style block and add
   *"match the previous images exactly in palette and line weight."*

---

## STYLE BLOCK — paste before every scene prompt

> Flat vector editorial illustration. Minimal and uncluttered with generous negative
> space. Limited warm palette: soft cream background, muted sage green, one warm
> terracotta accent, charcoal line work. Even, soft lighting; no harsh shadows; no
> gradients beyond a subtle wash. Clean confident line weight. **No text, no letters,
> no numbers, no logos. No human faces.** Calm, quiet, unhurried mood. 16:9
> composition, subject centered with wide margins.

**NEGATIVE (if the tool supports it):** text, letters, words, numbers, watermarks,
logos, faces, people, cluttered UI, neon colors, dramatic shadows, 3D render,
photorealism, glossy tech aesthetic, busy backgrounds.

## SCENE 1 — the problem
> A smartphone lying face-up on a plain kitchen counter. Its screen shows six blank
> stacked notification bars, overflowing past the top and bottom edges of the screen
> so the pile reads as endless. Beside the phone, a single sheet of paper half-buried
> under a small stack of unopened envelopes. Slight clutter, but calm — the
> composition should feel *quietly overwhelming*, not chaotic.

## SCENE 2 — what it does
> Six small simple icons arranged in a row across the top: an envelope, a calendar
> page, a document, a bell, a speech bubble, a photograph. Thin charcoal lines flow
> down from all six and converge into a narrow funnel in the middle of the frame.
> From the funnel's base, a single small clean card emerges at the bottom, blank and
> tidy. The visual argument is six-becomes-one.

## SCENE 3 — how it works
> A composition split into two halves by a thin vertical line. **Left half:** a
> precise mechanical checklist — five identical blank rows, each with a small tick
> mark, drawn geometrically with a ruler-straight feel, in muted sage. **Right half:**
> a fountain pen resting mid-stroke, having drawn one smooth flowing line across a
> blank card, organic and handwritten, in warm terracotta. The two halves should feel
> obviously different in character: left = mechanical and exact, right = human and
> expressive.

## SCENE 4 — what you get
> A quiet kitchen counter in soft early-morning light. A single phone lies face-up,
> its screen showing one small tidy card with three short blank lines. A coffee mug
> sits beside it. Everything else on the counter is clear. Spacious, restful, the
> visual opposite of scene 1.

## SCENE 5 — the limits
> Three simple rounded cards floating side by side with generous space between them,
> each holding one clear pictogram: **(1)** a small bar chart with a diagonal line
> struck through it; **(2)** a closed envelope with a small padlock resting on it;
> **(3)** a toggle switch clearly in the OFF position. Symmetrical, calm, evenly
> spaced. No text — labels are added in the editor.

## SCENE 6 — close
> Extreme minimalism. A single phone centered on an empty soft cream background,
> nothing else in frame. Its screen shows one small clean card containing three short
> blank lines and a simple circular clock face whose hands point to six o'clock.
> Maximum negative space; the quietest image in the set.
